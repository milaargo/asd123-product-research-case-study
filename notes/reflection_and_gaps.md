# Reflection, Limitations, and Research Gaps

This project taught me as much through its constraints as through its findings.

## What Went Wrong

### Direct user access was restricted

The original research plan could not be executed as intended.

I adapted by researching the ecosystem surrounding the child, but those perspectives were still proxies for direct observation of children using the product.

### Research began late in the sprint

The founder kickoff occurred five days into a three-week engagement.

That compressed recruitment, interviewing, synthesis, and design handoff into a little over one week.

### Success measures were not defined before handoff

The project produced design recommendations, but the team did not establish clear behavioral success metrics before development.

That made later attribution more difficult.

### Usability evidence became incomplete over time

A separate UI researcher conducted usability testing with five participants, but I no longer have the original test plan or underlying results.

Because those artifacts are unavailable, I do not report unsupported task-success rates or usability metrics.

## What I Would Do Differently

### Test earlier

I would test low-fidelity concepts before visual design matured.

### Define measurement before development

For each major recommendation, I would define the behavior expected to change and how it would be measured after launch.

For example:

- roadmap completion;
- repeat activity use;
- activity abandonment;
- time to next session;
- parent comprehension;
- reward interaction.

### Get closer to observed behavior

Interviews explained needs and context, but observational research could reveal behavior participants might not articulate.

I would look for ethical ways to observe children using ASD123 in context or conduct a comparable child-app interaction study if access remained restricted.

### Combine attitudes with behavior

Future work should combine:

- surveys;
- usability testing;
- observational research;
- task analysis;
- product analytics.

This would help distinguish what users say they value from what actually changes how they use the product.

## Current Research Gaps

The strongest unanswered questions are:

- Does the roadmap increase repeat use or activity completion?
- Do rewards improve engagement beyond novelty effects?
- Which onboarding variables meaningfully improve personalization?
- How do children use the product independently at home?
- Which accessibility controls are actually used by families?
- Where does the live product create friction that the prototype did not reveal?
